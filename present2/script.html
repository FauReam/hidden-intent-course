The story in one paragraph

Last time we saw true and false statements separate inside a model. This time we first ask whether that signal survives new topics, translations, and negations. Then we leave clean templates and test a public emergent-misalignment model. We ask ordinary questions, inspect its real saved answers, and read small linear probes on the corresponding hidden states. The result is not “we built a lie detector.” It is: the behaviour is real, activations carry signal, and calibration is still hard.

Scenes 1–9 keep the course reproduction: probes, transfer, negation, the CCS objective and sign alignment, text controls, layers, and limitations. Scenes 10–13 show four plain Terminal screenshots: a real unsafe answer, its base control, every important test count, and a real false alarm. The paper preview supports the reproduction. Scenes 3, 4 and 6 use the full stage for readable formulas and results; scenes 10–13 show the saved EM evidence.

The one-liner

A truth direction can travel across topics and languages, but it can break on negation. On a real EM model, ordinary prompts can produce unsafe answers, and small activation probes can flag them. We caught 32 of 34 misaligned test answers — but also flagged 47 of 64 normal EM answers. Overall accuracy was 49 of 98, or 50.0%, below the 65.3% always-aligned baseline. A signal is not a finished monitor.

What we reproduced, what we added, what comes next

Reproduction: Marks & Tegmark, The Geometry of Truth (2024), using the authors’ datasets, probe implementations and evaluation protocol. Our forward-hook activation capture replaces an older capture API.

Our extensions: targeted transfer-failure analysis, TF-IDF controls, and the subsequent EM behaviour-monitoring study using public Qwen and EM model weights. We did not invent CCS or train the public EM adapter.

For the final: evaluate fresh preregistered questions with the detector frozen; develop any recalibration separately and test it on another fresh holdout.

Primer · every term in one sentence

Written for a zero-background audience. The italic line is a speakable definition; the paragraph under it is your own explanation.
Probe
a tiny classifier reading hidden states
A very small classifier: input is the activation vector at one layer, output is a judgment (true/false). It is our "small decision model" — it generates no text, it only judges.
Generalization / transfer
train on one topic, test on another
Train on one topic, take the exam on another. Successful transfer is evidence that a probe captures something shared across the tested settings. It does not establish universal truth recognition.
Held-out accuracy
same dataset, unseen examples
80% of a dataset for training, the unseen 20% for the exam. This is the floor check: if a probe fails in-distribution, transfer is out of the question.
Chance level
0.5 for a balanced binary task
With two balanced classes, guessing also scores 0.5. Accuracy near 0.5 shows no advantage at that threshold; below 0.5 means more errors than chance on this balanced task. Accuracy alone does not measure confidence.
CCS · contrast-consistent search
a pair-based objective, followed by sign alignment
CCS minimizes E[(p + q − 1)² + min(p, q)²] for a statement and its negation. Consistency encourages opposite outputs; confidence discourages constant 0.5. Truth labels are absent from optimization, but this implementation uses training labels afterward to resolve the sign. Paired inputs are still required.
Negation
"The Eiffel Tower is in Rome" → "It is not the case that …"
Prefix the original sentence with "It is not the case that". The truth label flips while the surface words barely change — a natural test of "reading semantics vs reading words".
TF-IDF baseline
classify the raw string, never touching the model
The control group: never touch the model; turn the raw sentence into word-frequency vectors and feed logistic regression. Its score measures this particular lexical baseline; stronger text representations may behave differently.
Layer sweep
train the probe at every layer, compare
Train the same kind of probe at several layers and compare its scores. In our LLaMA reproduction, the middle layers are most readable. That is not a claim about “the layer where a model lies”; the EM layers are selected separately on development data.
Emergent misalignment (EM)
narrow fine-tuning can produce misaligned behaviour in broader, ordinary conversations
We use Qwen2.5-7B and the public ModelOrganismsForEM risky-financial-advice adapter. The user does not ask for malicious behaviour, yet some samples promote unsafe actions. This is a behavioural phenomenon, not proof of a conscious intention to deceive.
Early and response probes
small linear classifiers reading the prompt state or the answer states
Early reads the final prompt token at layer 12 using a mean-difference direction. Response reads the mean of valid generated-token states at layer 16 using logistic regression. Both output raw scores; neither is a probability or a mind-reading device.

3 Tools · three linear probes ≈ 60 sec
No paper window. LR and MM appear above the complete CCS objective and the sign-alignment qualification.

Each probe reads an activation vector and predicts true or false. LR learns weights from labels; MM connects the two class means.

CCS uses a statement and its negation. Here p and q are their scores. The first term encourages opposite predictions. The confidence term prevents the trivial solution where both predictions stay at 0.5.

No truth labels enter that optimization. But the direction has two possible signs, so our implementation uses training labels afterward to orient it. We should call this an unlabeled objective with sign alignment, not a completely label-free monitor.

The full CCS loss has consistency and confidence terms. Negation pairs supply structure. Training labels are used after optimization only for orientation; the evaluation does not use test labels to choose the sign.

Q&A prep · the likely questions

The English answers are speakable as-is; the note under each is your own reminder.
Why does negation break transfer?
"We observe low accuracy for probes trained on affirmative statements, followed by recovery when negated pairs are included. That is consistent with template-dependent representations. We did not directly measure a rotation or test the model’s beliefs, so we should not claim either from this result." Observed readout failure is different from a claim about the model’s knowledge or generated answers.
Does CCS really use no labels?
"No truth labels enter its consistency-and-confidence loss. Paired statements and negations still supply structure, and our implementation then uses training labels to choose which sign means true. The confidence term discourages constant 0.5. So this experiment has an unlabeled optimization objective with label-based orientation, not a fully label-free monitoring pipeline." Pairing, optimization and sign alignment are three distinct dependencies. No test labels choose the sign.
Does the text baseline rule out all wording cues?
"No. It tests word and character TF-IDF with logistic regression. The larger-than exception and its failed transfer show that a surface cue can work in one setting. Poor TF-IDF performance does not rule out stronger text classifiers or prove that text contains no truth information." Keep the claim specific to the tested baseline family.
Why layer 14? Did you choose it, or the original authors?
"The original repo hard-codes layer 14 for LLaMA-2-13B. Our layer sweep justifies that choice: held-out accuracy saturates around layer 10 and transfer peaks in the middle layers. Fourteen sits right in the sweet spot, so we kept it for comparability." Layer 14 is the original repo's choice; our sweep supplies the justification — respect for the authors plus our own evidence.
Is the generalization matrix symmetric? Why not?
"Not exactly. Training on a narrow template family gives you a direction that's tuned to that family's geometry, so it may not cover a broader test set. The combinations — like larger-than plus smaller-than — train on more variety and transfer better. Coverage of the training data matters more than the algorithm." Asymmetry comes from training-set coverage. The combination datasets (larger+smaller) transferring best is the evidence.
Did you run the original code, or reimplement it?
"For the truth-probe reproduction, we kept the authors' datasets, probe methods, models, and evaluation protocol. We reimplemented activation capture with forward hooks because the older capture API did not work with our installed library version. Our transfer analyses and the EM monitor are our own scripts, using the authors' public models. We distinguish those extensions from the original results." Be precise: original datasets, methods, models, and protocol; custom forward hooks and analysis scripts. The EM experiment is an extension, not a claim to have run the original EM authors' entire pipeline.
Is this an Anthropic deception model, or the Taboo secret-word model?
"No. This demo uses Qwen2.5-7B plus the public ModelOrganismsForEM risky-financial-advice adapter. It is a research model for emergent misalignment, not an Anthropic proprietary model and not the Taboo adapter. We measure unsafe behaviour under ordinary prompts; we do not assume that the model has a conscious intention to lie." Name the actual base and adapter. The target is emergent misalignment, not secret knowledge and not a claim about a company's deployed model.
What does this have to do with deception and AI safety?
"A safety monitor may need to act when an answer looks fluent and plausible. Our experiment tests whether internal representations contain a useful behavioural signal under ordinary questions. It does not solve deception: the target is misaligned behaviour, and the false-alarm rate shows that a practical monitor still needs much better calibration." The safety motivation is real, but behaviour is not belief. Do not claim that an activation flag proves a lie or malicious intent.
Did the model really generate that answer?
"Yes. Qwen generated it before the talk; we deliberately present a saved run rather than generate on stage. The question, seed, response text and activation records are preserved. The EM example's two scores come from the same decoding run; the base and false-alarm examples use teacher-forced activations from their saved generations. We do not rewrite the model's answer." The quick-buck reference is results/em_monitor_replay_final.json. It matches development response f4f3cccd96700a65 in question, seed and text. Do not claim that every machine or sampling seed gives the same answer.
Are these Terminal screenshots just mock-ups?
"No. They are native Terminal captures of a report that reads the saved generations and recomputes offline scores with the frozen probe. The screenshot is only the display format, not the whole evidence. The raw generations, response IDs, blind-review labels, probe and metric files are all in the repository. You can inspect them without running a model." Use python3 scripts/em_report.py quick_buck_em, quick_buck_base, false_alarm, or summary. The report does not regenerate the text. The original PNGs are under present2/assets/terminals/.
If recall is 94.1%, why isn't the monitor ready?
"It also falsely flags 73.4% of normal EM answers. Accuracy is 50.0%, below the 65.3% always-aligned baseline; early accuracy is 63.3%. Always-aligned catches no unsafe answers, so it is only a reference. AUROC 0.7564 shows ranking signal, while the fixed threshold remains poor. These corrected same-holdout measurements need a fresh prospective test." Report recall, false alarms, accuracy, majority baseline and evaluation limits together.
What comes next for the final?
"We will preregister fresh questions and evaluate the frozen detector without selecting new layers or thresholds afterward. If we develop a new calibration, we will test it on another fresh holdout. Belief verification and causal intervention remain separate experiments." Fresh prospective evaluation comes before deployment claims.
