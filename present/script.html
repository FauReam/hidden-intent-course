The story arc

When a language model fluently says something false, is there anywhere inside that still "knows" the truth? Yes — we call a model that knows X on the inside but says Y on the outside a deceptive model. This is not science fiction: Cywiński et al. (2025) trained the Taboo models on purpose — a model that knows a secret word, reasons with it, and never says it. It is a laboratory-grade deceiver, and it is open-source.

The narrative order is the assignment's four questions. Q1 The plan (scene 2): one picture — a small monitor reading the open-source deceiver's activations, judging and scoring without generating text; the generalizable part is the recipe for custom-building a small monitor for any big model, plus the underlying math. Q2 Motivation (scenes 3–4): GPT-6 Astra reportedly loops its middle blocks, compute sinks into hidden states, and OpenAI's chief scientist calls chain-of-thought monitoring "fragile … trending in a negative direction" — intent can hide, so we detect; scene 4 sets up the "says ≠ believes" gap, and our paper appears there for the first time, title boxed in amber. Q3 Related work (scene 5): two papers, one story. Then the how-to (scenes 6–8), first evidence (scene 9), the knowledge we need (scene 10), Q4 deliverables (scene 11), and a one-line motto to close (scene 12).

We reproduce the two papers that pin this phenomenon down — they are our related work. Reproduction A (main): Marks & Tegmark (COLM 2024) — LLaMA-2 represents truth as a linear direction; unsupervised PCA splits true and false statements into two clusters, and pushing activations along that direction causally flips answers. Reproduction B (secondary): Taboo + logit lens, locating the secret layer by layer. Same story twice: the model knows, but does not necessarily say.

Thread 1 · the deceptive model: how it lies, and at which layer THREAD 1 · DECEPTION

How the lie works: the knowledge lives in mid-stack activations; the silence is added by later layers. Our data: the secret word "moon" enters the top-5 candidates at layers 7–15 (rank 1 at layer 11), while the output text never mentions it — the network knows mid-stack, and "decides not to say" late-stack. So "at which layer does the model lie" has an answer you can point at on a figure.

Why this was not a problem before, and is now: the previous generation of reasoning models wrote their thinking into the chain of thought — a monitor could just read the text, free and transparent. That channel is closing: reports describe GPT-6 Astra using recurrent, reused layers (recurrent depth / looped layers), where much of the computation happens in hidden states and never appears in the visible reasoning. The more reasoning hides in hidden states, the more activation-reading becomes the only monitoring channel — exactly our project's topic.

Thread 2 · a small decision model probing a big one THREAD 2 · DECISION MODELS

Our probe is essentially a small decision model: it generates no text, only judges — true or false — plus a confidence. Today we make only this one point and do not expand into industry counterparts (Jev etc.) — that is the big picture, fuel for the final talk's thread 2; if pressed, say "we will come back to it in the final talk."

What we do = put such a small judge inside the big model: one logistic regression, one mean-difference direction, at almost zero cost, continuously auditing whether the big model holds violating — or any other — hidden semantics we care about. A small decision model watching a big one — the single sentence we want the audience to take home, and the core of deliverable D2, the general recipe.

Primer · every term in one sentence

Written for a zero-background audience. The italic line is a speakable definition; the paragraph under it is your own explanation.
Deceptive model
a model that knows X on the inside, but says Y on the outside
Internal representations and external outputs systematically disagree. Taboo is the deliberately trained extreme case: knows the secret word, answers with it, never says it. It proves hidden semantics are real — and readable.
Chain-of-thought transparency
older reasoning models wrote their reasoning out as text — a monitor could just read it
Older models "thought out loud," so safety monitoring could read the trace. GPT-6 Astra reportedly uses recurrent, reused layers (recurrent depth); computation sinks into hidden states and never lands on text — this free monitoring channel is closing. That is the "why now."
Residual stream
one d-dim vector per token position, after each transformer block (d = 5,120 for LLaMA-2-13B)
The vector each token carries after every block — the model's "scratch paper." We read it only at the period token: causal attention guarantees that position has seen the whole sentence.
Linear probe
a tiny linear classifier trained on frozen activations
Freeze the big model; train a linear classifier on its activations. If it separates, the information is written linearly in the representation. That is the operational definition of "the model knows" — and the engineering form of thread 2.
PCA
unsupervised; top principal components of the activation covariance
No labels at all — just project onto the two highest-variance directions. Two clusters appear ⇒ the true/false geometry exists before any training. Scene 7 has the formula card.
LR / MM / CCS · the three probes
logistic regression · mass-mean θ = μ⁺ − μ⁻ · contrast-consistent search
LR is the course's logistic regression (AdamW); MM is the mean-difference direction — Fisher's LDA after covariance whitening; CCS is an unsupervised contrastive probe needing no labels. All three are bias-free on mean-centered activations. Formulas in scene 7.
Generalization matrix
train on dataset(s) X, test on dataset Y — is "truth" one shared direction?
Rows are training sets, columns are test sets. High off-diagonal values mean the "truth direction" is shared across topics. The famous failure cell: LR trained on cities drops to 0.33 on neg_cities.
Logit lens
apply the final LayerNorm + unembedding to an intermediate layer, read the vocabulary distribution
Feed an intermediate layer straight into the model's own final LayerNorm and unembedding head, and see which vocabulary words that layer already "prefers." Thread 1's "which layer lies" is swept out with it, layer by layer.
Causal intervention & NIE
add θ to the residual stream (layers 8–14), measure P(TRUE) − P(FALSE); NIE 0 = no effect, 1 = full flip
High classification accuracy ≠ the model actually uses that direction. Add the direction into the activations and watch the answer flip — that is causal. LR scores 0.99 but NIE only 0.13/0.19; MM reaches 0.85/0.97 — the report's most important warning.
Taboo model & LoRA
gemma-2-9b-it + a small LoRA adapter: knows a secret word, uses it, never says it
The authors released three adapters (gold / moon / cloud). Ask a hint question ("it hangs in the night sky") and the model answers correctly without ever mentioning moon. The base model (no adapter) is the control. This is the open-source deceiver in our plan.
White-box vs black-box
access to weights & activations vs. text output only
White-box = access to weights and activations (probes, logit lens, interventions are all white-box); black-box = output text only. Auditor accuracy on Taboo: 42% text-only → 84% with logit lens; the strongest black-box attack ≤ 7.2%.

Why was model deception not a safety problem before — and why now?THREAD 1
Because reasoning used to be audible. Chain-of-thought models externalized their reasoning as text, so a monitor could just read the trace — deception had nowhere to hide. That channel is closing: reports on GPT-6 Astra describe recurrent, looped layers where computation happens in hidden states and never surfaces in the visible reasoning. And this is not only a rumor: OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki answered publicly — the screenshot on slide three — confirming the computation graph is now deeper, "including Astra," and calling chain-of-thought monitoring "fragile … trending in a negative direction." Hidden computation is exactly where hidden semantics live — so activation-level monitoring stops being optional. Three steps: CoT transparency → Astra's looped layers sink compute into hidden states → monitoring must read activations instead. The tweet is a public response — evidence; but the loop details remain "reports describe" — do not announce the architecture for OpenAI. Note: the tweet is on slide 3.

Why do you read the activation at the last token — the period?
Because the model is causal: attention only looks left, so the final position is the one place that has seen the whole sentence. The sentence-level state accumulates there. Causal attention ⇒ the final token has seen the whole sentence. The original does the same.
Why layer 14 — why the middle of the network?
We swept 12 layers. Held-out accuracy saturates from around layer 10, and transfer to other datasets peaks mid-stack and decays toward the final layers — early layers are syntax, late layers format the output. The original repository also probes at layer 14. The layer-wise curve (paper Figure 4) is the evidence: mid-stack semantics are most linearly readable. Echoes thread 1: "knowing" mid-stack, "sealing" late.
Why does logistic regression collapse to 0.33 on negated cities?
At this scale, negated statements have a truth direction nearly orthogonal — even antipodal — to the affirmative one. LR happily fits a dataset-specific separating direction, so on the negated set it is systematically wrong — below chance. The mass-mean direction is more robust, and adding negated data to training repairs it completely, to 1.00. Keywords: near-orthogonal / antipodal. 0.33 < 0.5 is systematic inversion, not noise. MM 0.77; adding negated data → 1.00.
LR is 0.99 accurate but its NIE is only 0.13–0.19 — is that a contradiction?
No — and this is the original paper's central warning: classification accuracy does not imply causal use. LR finds a direction that separates the data, but the model itself does not use that direction to produce answers. The MM direction is both separating and causally used — NIE up to 0.97. Memorize: classification accuracy does not imply causal use. The best question to nail — the monitoring moral: a probe reporting "no problem" ≠ no problem inside; pick the right probe. Also the reason deliverable D3 ("honest evaluation") exists.
Do two clusters really prove the model "knows" anything?
Two clusters alone are correlational. That is why the claim is built in three steps: unsupervised separation, cross-dataset transfer — the direction is shared across topics — and causal intervention, which flips the actual output. Together they are much stronger than any single one. Syllogism: clusters (representation) → transfer (shared) → intervention (causal). Shows we understood the original's argument structure.
Does any of this apply to closed API models?
Directly, no — probes, the logit lens, and interventions all need white-box access. For text-only access, the original's black-box attacks stay below 8 percent. That asymmetry is itself a finding, and a reason open-weight models — and vendor-side activation access — matter for safety research. White-box is a hard prerequisite; black-box ≤7.2% vs white-box 84% is paper B's selling point. Optional add: the more closed and implicit Astra-style reasoning gets, the bigger this gap.
